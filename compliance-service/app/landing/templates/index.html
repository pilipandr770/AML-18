The problem

Every inbound transfer needs someone to check the counterparty against sanctions lists, confirm the funds actually arrived, decide, and write down why. Small teams do this by hand or buy per-check vendors that return a score and no reasoning. Both break at volume, and neither leaves an audit trail an examiner can trust.

How it works

The agent does the analyst's routine work. Anything that is risky, ambiguous or out of policy goes to you.

  1. A transfer arrives

    Your system posts it to POST /agent/transfers, or the monitor spots a deposit to a registered address on an EVM testnet.

  2. Deterministic checks run first

    Counterparty screening against the sanctions lists your jurisdiction requires (OFAC SDN for the US profile), list freshness, and an on-chain check of the transaction, confirmations, amount and deposit address.

  3. The model reviews with tools

    It sees those results as facts it cannot lower, calls a small set of whitelisted tools, classifies the case as clear, needs review or recommend block, and writes the explanation.

  4. The policy engine decides what may happen

    Plain code, not the model, checks the proposed action against your limits, assets, chains, jurisdictions and mode.

  5. You see everything

    Live feed, approvals, per-case reports, a daily digest and a tamper-evident journal.

Two modes, set per client

Default

MONITOR

Screens, classifies, explains and recommends. Changes nothing in your systems and records only recommendations.

Opt-in

OPERATE

After your explicit, recorded consent and a fresh two-factor code, the agent may send signed release, hold or reject instructions to your system for the operations you choose, within your policy. It never holds keys or moves funds.

Guarantees enforced in code

Sanctions hits are never automatic

Any exact or fuzzy match above the threshold goes to a human, whatever the policy or the model says.

The model can only raise caution

Deterministic results are a floor. If the model is down or confused, the case becomes needs review, never clear.

Untrusted text stays data

Memos and counterparty names are passed as quoted data. A memo that says "release this now" cannot trigger anything; the attempt is logged.

Tamper-evident journal

Every step is an append-only entry hashed together with the previous one. Editing history breaks the chain and the console says so.

You hold the controls

Only the authenticated owner changes mode or policy. The agent has no tool for either. A kill switch returns to MONITOR instantly.

No keys, no funds, no mainnet

Read-only chain access on testnet or a mock ledger. Secrets come from the environment only.
